# AI Use Case Intake Procedure

## When to submit a use case

Submit an intake form before building or buying any AI solution that goes beyond personal
productivity with approved tools, for example an assistant grounded on a team's documents, an
automated workflow, or an agent that can act on a system.

## The intake form

The form asks for the business problem, the users, the information involved and its
classification, the expected benefit in hours or quality, the estimated running cost, and a
named business owner who is accountable for the solution once it is live.

## Risk levels and approval

The AI Office assigns a risk level. Minimal-risk use cases, such as drafting help over public
information, are approved by the AI Office alone. Limited-risk use cases need the business
owner and the Information Security team to agree. High-risk use cases, such as anything that
affects individuals or processes restricted information, are approved by the Digital Governance
Board.

## Timelines

The AI Office acknowledges every intake within three working days and gives a first assessment
within ten working days. High-risk use cases are scheduled for the next Board meeting.

## Costs

Every unit has a free monthly allowance on the approved tools. Usage above the allowance, and
any dedicated infrastructure, is paid by the unit that owns the use case. Cost estimates are
reviewed after the first three months of operation.

## From prototype to production

A prototype may run for up to eight weeks with named test users. Moving to production requires
an evaluation report, a support model agreed with the service desk, and documentation that lets
another team maintain the solution.
